## Overview

**Purpose**. The purpose of the final project is to give you an opportunity to deepen your skills and understanding of the ideas taught in the class by applying them again, this time in a team setting. 

**Expectations**. All members of the team are expected to contribute roughly equally, and to engage in *all* activities. This means that you are welcome to divide responsibilities so that team members work on different areas of the project, but you may *not* divide by activity: every member must be involved in problem framing, designing, implementation, and so on. At the same time, you may choose to assign primary *oversight* of a given activity to a single team member. All members of the team should expect to receive the same grade unless there are major inequities in effort; we hope this won't happen and should be notified early on if it seems to be happening so that we can help remedy it.

**Activities**. The activities that comprise the final project will be the same as the activities that you have already performed in the personal project, with the addition of (1) a few new activities related to team management and (2) a consideration of ethical issues. For those activities that are not new, this document does not explain the activities and only specifies the deliverables; you can refer to the earlier assignment documents and rubrics for guidance and advice on those activities. The new activities are as follows:
- **Team contract**. At the very start of your project, you'll agree on a contract for how you'll work together as a team to minimize the likelihood of misunderstandings.
- **Ethical analysis**. As part of your problem framing, you'll conduct an ethical analysis using a framework called *value sensitive design*, identifying some of the possible undesirable consequences of the kinds of app that address the problem you're working on, and extracting lessons that are relevant for your own design.
- **Pitches and critiques**. Prior to submitting your problem framing document, your team will present a "pitch" in person to the whole class for feedback on your project idea. Each student in the class will provide a brief critique of one project, so that each project will receive three or four peer critiques, in addition to feedback from the teaching staff.
- **Project plan**. As part of your design document, you will include a plan that explains how you will develop your project incrementally; what risks you are most concerned about; and how you will mitigate them.

**Advice**. We hope that this project will be a wonderful experience for you; that you'll build an exciting app, do creative and thoughtful design work, and learn a lot. With that in mind, we offer the following advice:
- **Engage with us**. Your teaching staff are here to help, so make good use of us! Come to your mentoring meetings with your TAs well prepared with a clear articulation of the problems you're currently facing, the issues you'd like advice on, and your plans going forward. When hard design questions arise, feel free to post questions on Commons. The lecturers are also available and eager to help. 
- **Be ambitious but control risk**. This project is a chance to do something exciting, so be ambitious. But work hard from the start to keep your project focused and avoid including non-essential concepts: better to have one great concept than a dozen mediocre ones. If your design involves some aspect that might fail (for example, use of an API or a technology that might not work as expected), make sure to include a fall-back option in your design.
- **Stick to your plan**. Don't let your team get behind. If you find that some activity is taking much longer than expected, then revisit your plan and consider cutting back or changing directions rather than just forging ahead and hoping for the best.
- **Collaborate fully**. Take the time to meet with your teammates in person, and to stay in regular contact. You can assign tasks and do them independently so long as you sync up frequently, but you might also consider getting together to work for camaraderie and to bounce ideas off each other.
- **Platform**. You are required to use the sync engine framework, but you are welcome to augment it with additional technologies (such as CSS frameworks, external APIs and services, etc).
- **Reusing and sharing concepts**. Feel free to reuse designs and code from your personal projects in your team project, including concepts that were implemented by any student in the class or by the teaching staff. You are encouraged to reuse the designs and implementations of concepts from [Commons](https://github.com/mit-sdg/commons), the class LMS, and from the [Concept Box](https://github.com/61040-fa25/conceptbox) demonstration project. If you develop a concept in the course of your team project that you think might be of interest to other teams, post a message about it on the [Commons forum](https://class.mit-sdg.dev) and you will receive extra credit if the concept is used by another team!

## Repos & submissions

**Repositories**. Each team should create one GitHub repository for their design and implementation work; it can be owned by one of the team members, with the other team members as collaborators. The other team members can then clone the repo and push changes to it. We recommend that you use a separate branch for each task or feature, making regular commits, which are pushed to the central repo; then you can open pull requests to merge from the feature branch into the main branch. The individual team members should continue to use their own personal repositories for reflections.

**Document format**. All non-code artifacts should be in markdown format, with any diagrams or screenshots inserted as linked images.

**Submissions**. As before, submissions will be made using Commons, providing a commit hash URL to mark the version of the repository to be graded (with the exception of the team proposal which is submitted as a form) . No slack days or lateness are permitted in final project deliverables.

## Final project phases
### Team proposal

Your first task will be to propose your team membership by submitting a form (one per team) that lists the intended members. If you don't have a team in mind, submit a form by yourself and we'll find you a match. Both teams and individuals will indicate what kind of problem they have in mind so we can match appropriately.

Each team will have four members (with perhaps one or two exceptions if the class roster is not divisible by four). You can propose a full team of four member, or fewer, and we will find additional team members for you.

Submit the form [here]().

### Team contract

Before you begin your work as a team, we ask you to agree on a “team contract.” We’ve found that such contracts take only a little time, but reduce the chance that projects are derailed by misunderstandings and disagreements. The team contract should be the result of an open and candid discussion amongst team members about your individual aspirations and commitments. Be brief and to the point; approximately half a page should suffice.

You should address the following questions:
- The expected level of achievement and effort for each team member (what grades do you expect to get? how much time do you plan to put in?);
- Personal goals for each team member (building a great app? learning a particular skill?);
- Frequency, length and location of team meetings;
- How quality of work will be maintained (reviewing each others’ code? team review meetings? pair programming?);
- How tasks will be assigned, and what to do if deadlines are missed;
- How decisions will be made and any disagreements resolved.

Because of its potentially personal nature, you should not place your team contract in your team repo. Instead, you should place it in the personal repo of one of the team members and submit that.

### Project updates   

You will meet with your TA mentors weekly to get advice and feedback on the progress of your project. Every team member is expected to attend every meeting. If for some reason, you are unable to come to a meeting, you should let your mentor know in advance.

It is *your* job to structure the meetings and make sure that they are helpful to you; you should think of your mentor as a consultant rather than a grader. For each meeting, you should come prepared with: a brief report on your progress since the last meeting; a list of problems or issues you want help with; an agenda for the meeting (what order you want to discuss things in, and how long each will take). At the meeting, you should write down your plan for the following week and any decisions you made.

Deliverables associated with each meeting:
- **Agenda**: topics to be discussed with time allocated to each
- **Progress report**: how last week went, alignment to plan
- **Design changes**: what changes were made to the design since the last meeting
- **Issues**: problems and issues to address, with a sentence or two explaining each
- **Plans & decisions** (completed after): plans for next week and decisions made

You should have a separate folder in your team repo for your mentor meetings. You can organize these deliverables as you please; you might, for example, have a single markdown file for each meeting with sections for each area. There is no official submission, but the quality of these deliverables will be taken into account at the end in determining a portion of your team's grade.

Relevant rubrics:
- [Professionalism rubric](../rubrics/professionalism.md)
- [Project management rubric](../rubrics/project-management.md)

### Project pitch

Each team will deliver a 4-minute presentation in person to the entire class. It is up to each team how to present, but we recommend that you prepare 3-5 slides and that you share the talking. 

The content of the pitch is a dry run for your problem framing, and an opportunity to get feedback early on. It should include at least:
- **The bad situation**: that you've identified and why it matters
- **Some solution ideas**: an outline, or some ingredients
- **What you'd like help with**: risks, concerns, etc

The purpose of the project pitch is to encourage you to get going quickly on a project idea, and to get feedback with time to adjust your direction. It will also be graded, according to the rubric. There is no deliverable to be handed in.

Relevant rubrics:
- [Project pitching rubric](../rubrics/project-pitching.md)

### Peer critique

Each of you will be assigned individually the task of critiquing another team project. You will provide critiques of three separate artifacts: the in-class pitch, the problem framing, and the design. In each case, your critique is due **three days** after the deadline for the artifact itself.

You should structure each critique in three parts:
- **I like**. Some aspects of the artifact that you like, and why.
- **I wish**. Some aspects of the artifact that you wish were different in some way.
- **I wonder**. Some aspects you are wondering about; perhaps some suggestions for a simplification, a different approach, or whatever.

You should expect to each spend a few hours in total on this, and to write no more than half a page in total for each critique. In grading this, we will consider primarily how *helpful* your critique is likely to be the receiving team.

Your critique should be placed in your personal repo and submitted like a regular assignment.

### Problem framing

The problem framing is like the one you did for the personal project, augmented with the ethics analysis using VSD. Since the ethical analysis is new, a full explanation of the steps involved is provided in a [separate document](./final-project-ethical-analysis). 

Deliverables:
- **Domain**: an explanation of the domain, or general area, in which the problem lies.
- **Bad situations**: one or more bad situations that arise in the domain and that call for a design intervention.
- **Evidence**: evidence to corroborate the claim that the bad situations are impactful.
- **Workarounds and comparables**: workarounds that people currently use to mitigate the bad situations, and comparable solutions if they exist.
- **Solution sketch**: an outline of a solution.
- **Ethical analysis**: an identification of stakeholders and the impacts they might experience in the short and long term.

Relevant rubrics:
- [Problem framing rubric](../rubrics/problem-framing.md)
- [Technical writing rubric](../rubrics/technical-writing.md)
- [Professionalism rubric](../rubrics/professionalism.md)

### Design

The design is like the one you did for the personal project, augmented with the development plan.

*The deliverables are:*

- **Problem framing**: A revision of your problem framing to reflect your latest understanding
- **Concept design**: A collection of concept specifications and reactions; include a **note** section in each concept and sync to explain any non-obvious design decisions or to mark issues to be resolved
- **User journey**: a narrative that follows a single stakeholder as they encounter the problem and use your designed app to address it.

- **Visual design study**: two slides (which can be inserted into markdown as images) that establish the _"look and feel"_ of your app by assembling a range of sources and annotating them to establish a visual style with typographic, color and layout.

- **Development plan**: A table showing what you expect to deliver at various stages, including at least the two checkpoints; an **assignment of responsibilities** to team members; a discussion of the **key risks** you face and how you will mitigate them (and your fallback option will be if something turns out to be unimplementable).

### Checkpoints: Alpha and Beta

For each checkpoint, in addition to submitting the code and updated designs, you will submit the following (1) a public URL pointing to your deployed app; (2) a screen recording showing the current state of your app, with an audio narration explaining some key features, and (3) a new version of the development plan explaining what progress you've made, what remains to be done, and how your plans might have changed.

Your alpha checkpoint should be an MVP, as for your personal project. Your beta checkpoint should be a functionally complete version of your implementation. Both should be deployed.

Deliverables are submitted as follows:
- **Source code, updated designs and updated development plan**: in the team repo, provided as a Git commit hash
- **Deployed app**: a separate link to the running, deployed app
- **Screen recording**: a separate link to a video recording, but also linked in the readme of the submitted repo
