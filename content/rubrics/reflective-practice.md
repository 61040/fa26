title: Reflective Practice
---
Core Criteria for Reflective Practice

- **Description of Experience:** Clearly states what happened without getting lost in trivial details.

- **Critical Analysis:** Examines why things happened, challenges assumptions, and questions personal actions.

- **Connection to Theory:** Links the experience to course concepts, readings, or professional standards.

- **Meaning-Making and Growth:** Articulates what was learned and how future behavior or thinking will change
Performance Levels

1. Exemplary (Proficient)

- **Description:** Gives a concise, relevant summary of the event.

- **Analysis:** Deeply questions personal reactions and looks at the situation from multiple viewpoints.

- **Theory:** Seamlessly weaves course concepts or outside literature into the reflection.

- **Growth:** Identifies specific, actionable insights and sets clear goals for future practice.

2. Developing (Satisfactory)

- **Description:** Explains the event, but includes too much irrelevant detail or misses key context.

- **Analysis:** Stays mostly on the surface level; describes what happened rather than analyzing why.

- **Theory:** Mentions a course concept, but fails to connect it meaningfully to the actual experience.

- **Growth:** States a vague lesson learned without a clear plan for future change.

3. Beginning (Needs Improvement)

- **Description:** The event is unclear, confusing, or missing vital details.

- **Analysis:** Merely complains or praises without any critical thought or self-awareness.

- **Theory:** Ignores course concepts or uses them incorrectly.

- **Growth:** Shows no evidence of new understanding or future action.
