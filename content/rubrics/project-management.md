---
title: Rubric for Project Management
---
Project management is a big topic. This rubric addresses only the very limited aspects of project management that are expected within the final project of the class.

A successfully managed final project requires:
- **Engagement**. All team members should attend all meetings with mentors, and should engage in constructive and respectful discussion with each other and with the mentor.
- **Planning**. The project plan should be thoughtful and realistic, paying attention to the risks that are likely to arise and assessing the relative sizes of tasks realistically. Roles should be assigned to team members fairly, and explicitly. When the team misses a deadline, the plan should be adjusted accordingly.
- **Agendas**. Each mentor meeting (and ideally each team meeting) should have a clear agenda worked out in advance and shared with all members before the meeting starts, listing the topics of discussion and the time alotted to each. 
- **Tracking**. All team members should report progress and difficulties so that all team members are aware of what tasks remain and what challenges need to be addressed.

Common pitfalls:
- **Imbalance**. Some team members dominate discussions and activities and others participate only minimally.
- **Missing meetings**. A team member fails to turn up for a mentoring meeting and does not notify the mentor in advance.
- **Lack of preparation**. Team members arrive at mentoring meeting without a properly prepared agenda.
- **Vague plans**. The project plan does not have clear, measurable milestones.

Competency levels:
- **Deficient**. The team seems to be a collection of individuals who are barely working together.
- **Emergent**. The team members are working together and making project plans and agendas, but they are often too vague or unclear to support definite assessment and action.
- **Competent**. The project plan and agendas are sufficiently concrete and relevant to guide the project, and the team responds thoughtfully and decisively when the project does not go according to plan, recording the issues and decisions appropriately in the agenda, and adjusting the project plan in a sensible way. 
- **Expert**. Team members not only use the project plan and agendas effectively, but also anticipate problems in advance. They have candid and constructive discussions considering different options when addressing challenges.
