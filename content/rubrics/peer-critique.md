---
title: Rubric for Peer Critique
---
A peer critique should:
- **Prioritize**. Start the critique between big picture issues and only then move to smaller concerns.
- **Be direct**. Get to the point. Be candid about perceived flaws and limitations, and point explicitly to relevant features.
- **Balance praise and criticism**. Highlight what is good in addition to problems so the author knows what strengths to build on and retain.
- **Be constructive**. Adopt a helpful and constructive tone, and whenever possible, offer suggestions when criticizing for how to remedy the problem.
- **Be respectful**.  Direct all criticism toward the work itself, never toward the person.

Common pitfalls:
- **Vagueness**. Criticisms that are vague and general are not usually helpful.
- **Nitpicking**. It's fine to note small issues (such as typos), but they should not dominate the critique if there are more substantive issues to raise.
- **Bias**. Some decisions and criteria are subjective, so be wary of criticism that essentially demands conformance with the critic's point of view.

Competency levels:
- **Deficient**. The critique does not provide any useful substantive feedback, or is too vague or poorly written to be intelligible.
- **Emergent**. The critique makes some valid points but focuses on unimportant issues, or fails to address the key ideas.
- **Competent**. The critique contains a thoughtful analysis of the artifact as a whole, points clearly to problems and merits, and makes helpful suggestions for improvement.
- **Expert**. In addition to meeting the criteria for competency, the critique provides some valuable insights that have the potential to reshape the future of the artifact.
