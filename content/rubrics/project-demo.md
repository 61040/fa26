---
title: Rubric for Project Demos
---

A demo of a software project should be:
- **Situated**. The demo starts by explaining, perhaps very briefly, what the context of use is.
- **Informative**. It should be clear to the audience what the project is for (what problem it solves) and how it works.
- **Compelling**. The demo should convey the novel and exciting aspects of the project, and convince the audience that they are worthy of attention.
- **Dramatic**. A demo should tell a story that follows a dramatic path, rather than being a series of unconnected interactions.
- **Easy to follow**. It should be obvious what is being done at each point in the demo, and why.
- **Populated**. The app has been populated in advance so that the data shown appears realistic, simulating how the app would appear once successful and widely deployed.
- **Complete**. The demo should include sufficient functionality (a full enough user journey) to show not just that the app runs but that it fulfills its intended purpose.

Common pitfalls:
- **Wasted steps**. The demo spends time on routine functionality (such as account registration) that is not core to the app's function.
- **Fake data**. The app has been populated with data that looks fake or unconvincing. Worse, fields are filled with meaningless placeholder text so that the audience has trouble even understanding how a particular field would be used.
- **Too slow or fast**. The demo is too slow because of application delays or watching the demonstrator type, or too fast to allow the audience to assimilate a screen and understand what's going on.
- **Missing functionality**. The demo misses functionality that is essential to understanding the value of the app. For example, a demo of an app designed for multi-user interactions might show only a single user; an app designed for data analysis might show only the entry of the data and not its usage.
- **Flat**. The demo does not build up to a moment of gratification but simply takes the audience through a series of steps without indicating which are significant and why.

Competency levels:
- **Deficient**. It is unclear what the app does or why.
- **Emergent**. The purpose and basic function of the app are mostly conveyed, but the demo is not compelling and does not convey a realistic usage.
- **Competent**. The demo compellingly conveys the purpose and key ideas of the app, and uses realistic data and interactions to give a sense of why it might succeed in practice.
- **Expert**. The demo is fun to watch and has a dramatic quality to it, revealing a novel solution or elegant piece of functionality. There is a narrative arc to the demo that makes it easy for the user to know where they are and where they are going. The demo conveys clearly what the problem is and why it matters, and gives the impression that the app offers a viable solution to it.
