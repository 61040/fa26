---
title: Rubric for Project Pitching
---
A project pitch should be:
- **Clear**. It should be obvious to the audience what problem is being solved and what the purported solution is.
- **Compelling**. The pitch should persuade the audience that the problem matters and is worth solving, and that the solution is novel and plausible.
- **Honest**. A pitch is not an advertisement; it should be honest about potential pitfalls and challenges.

Common pitfalls:
- **Inadequate background**. The pitch assumes knowledge of some domain, so those unfamiliar with it cannot properly understand the problem or solution.
- **No bad situation**. The pitch does not present a real problem a a concrete, bad situation. In the worst case, it simply presents the desire for a particular technological innovation as if it were problem ("the problem is to provide LLM based summarization of email messages").
- **Vagueness**. The pitch explains the problem and solution in vague terms rather than using concrete scenarios to explain them.
- **Lack of focus**. The pitch actually presents multiple problems, or an excessively complex solution, rather than one well defined problem and a simple solution to it.
- **Lack of professionalism**. The presenters don't introduce themselves, and their names are not listed on a title slide.

Competency levels:
- **Deficient**. Neither the problem nor the solution is clear.
- **Emergent**. A problem and solution are articulated, but they lack clarity or are not compelling.
- **Competent**. The problem is compellingly described and the proposed solution is appropriate. Relevant risks and concerns are raised.
- **Expert**. The description of the problem shows insight that would elude typical observers, and successfully identifies the problem's essence. The solution is presented in a way that makes it seem not only effective but also simple and elegant. The presentation is honest and thorough in terms of limitations, risks and concerns.
