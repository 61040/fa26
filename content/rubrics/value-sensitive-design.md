---
title: Rubric for Value-Sensitive Design
---

A good ethical analysis should have these qualities:

- **Stakeholders.** The analysis identifies people affected by the app, including indirect stakeholders who do not use it or cannot choose whether to be affected. It explains how particular aspects of the design affect them.
- **Time.** The analysis considers how consequences may change as people adapt to the technology, repurpose it, or choose not to use it.
- **Pervasiveness.** The analysis considers how widespread adoption could change behavior, social expectations, or the surrounding system, rather than simply exposing more people to the same effect.
- **Values.** The analysis explains which values design choices support or threaten, including relevant tensions between values or stakeholders' interests.
- **Design responses.** Each response follows from a specific observation and explains a concrete change to the design. The analysis considers opportunities to create benefits beyond the intended users as well as ways to mitigate harm.

## Common pitfalls

- **Generic concerns.** Statements such as "privacy is important" could apply to almost any app and do not explain what could happen in this design.
- **Only considering users.** The analysis overlooks people affected by the app who do not choose to use it.
- **Stopping at immediate effects.** The analysis assumes that behavior and social expectations will remain unchanged as adoption grows.
- **Listing values without examining tensions.** The analysis names desirable values without explaining whose interests are served or what a design choice might compromise.
- **Only looking for harm.** The analysis misses opportunities to benefit stakeholders or support important values.
- **Responses without substance.** Suggestions such as "be fair" or "protect privacy" do not explain what would change.

## Competency levels

- **Deficient.** The analysis is missing or does not connect VSD observations to the proposed app. Design responses are absent or unintelligible.
- **Emergent.** The analysis identifies relevant concerns but mainly considers immediate effects on intended users. It names broader effects or values without explaining how they arise. Design responses are generic or weakly connected to the observations.
- **Competent.** The analysis considers the four VSD dimensions and explains concrete consequences beyond immediate use. It identifies affected stakeholders and values, including relevant tensions, and connects its observations to specific, reasoned design responses. It considers opportunities for benefit as well as harm.
- **Expert.** The analysis meets the competent criteria and reveals significant, non-obvious consequences or opportunities—for example, how widespread adoption changes expectations, or how a design affects people who cannot opt out. Its design responses thoughtfully address competing values or create benefits beyond the intended users, while acknowledging remaining tradeoffs.

The lecture examples illustrate these qualities; they are not a checklist of consequences that every project must exhibit.
